# Wallet SMS account isolation: implementation boundaries

Status: **work in progress; NO-GO for real SMS or release**. This design applies only to wallet and bank SMS data. Existing production capture, processing, review and notifications still use ownerless records. Existing legacy records are **not locked yet** and may still be shown, sent or removed by other paths. Queue-key collisions and mismatched item IDs now receive partial holds rather than intentional overwrites, but check/write/remove is not atomic across runtimes and does not establish ownership. Age-based candidate/quarantine deletion was replaced with a hold for aged or undated records. A persisted content-free marker makes an observed hold sticky across later clock changes, but a backward clock change before first observation or a failed marker write can bypass it; checks and mutations are not atomic across runtimes. It does not lock recent records. The new cipher is not wired to those paths.

## Security boundary

- A sender ID, SMS content, current UI account, or a bound token read after an asynchronous delay cannot establish which account owned an earlier message. Historical ownership must never be inferred.
- New records need a live authenticated account/session snapshot at the capture linearization point, not the account active when a worker later wakes up. The server-issued token/owner pair is an ingredient, not proof that the session is still current; a stale pair surviving sign-out or an account switch must not authorize capture.
- The backend's wallet-only routes require `X-Wallet-Owner` to match the authenticated principal. This is a precondition, not a credential or evidence of message ownership. The old routes still accept missing owners. A new client must not silently fall back to old routes, including when a server is too old, unavailable, or returns 404/428.
- Already-started network/provider work cannot generally be recalled once delivered. Define and test what is permitted before and after the sign-out linearization point; do not promise cancellation of remote work already accepted. Account A's item must never be sent with account B's credentials or shown/modified in B's UI.

## Current isolated crypto groundwork

`WalletOwnedPayloadCipher` seals the canonical owner UUID and payload with AES-GCM and authenticates the exact queue preference key as associated data. Moving a ciphertext between item IDs or candidate/review/transaction queues fails authentication. `WalletOwnedQueueCipher` uses a separate Android Keystore alias and rejects legacy, unknown and malformed envelope versions, with no plaintext fallback. The old arbitrary-plaintext encryption method channel cannot use that distinct alias. These classes are internal and unused by production paths; their existence **does not establish the account/session boundary or protect old items**. Replay of an earlier valid ciphertext under the same key is not prevented by this format. Android Keystore, crash, process and physical-device behavior remain unverified.

Flutter now serializes selected authentication transitions within one controller and suppresses older login/restore results after sign-out begins. `HomeShell` also supplies an observed account/generation guard to its wallet queue flushes, and profile-state updates check that generation. This is not a native capture fence or a cross-engine lock; already-started network/storage work, other callers and engines can still race or stall. It does not prove the ownership of a queued record.

Review UI and review operations also use the observed Flutter controller/account/generation and a content-free transition signal to avoid publishing cached results or using stale actions after a locally observed transition. The sheet hides on a subsequent UI rebuild, not instantaneously at the display level. This does not establish review ownership: ownerless records can still be loaded for a later account, other controllers and engines do not share this fence, and already-started operations cannot be recalled. The underlying review fence passed hosted Flutter verification on `9f31817`; follow-up widget tests are pending hosted verification.

`WalletCaptureGate` is a separate, unwired process-local prototype: it begins inactive, issues one-use reservations for the active in-memory session, rejects a reservation after a transition, and orders its synchronous capture callback against invalidation. It cannot authenticate credentials, choose the correct SMS arrival boundary, preserve rejected captures, make writes durable or coordinate multiple instances/processes. Callback re-entry is rejected, but this is not a replacement for the required native authority, owner-bound storage or cross-runtime queue protocol. It must not be wired in isolation as a substitute for the requirements below.

For new insertions, the strict wallet transaction routes now recheck the authenticated session, and the authenticated ingest-token row where relevant, under the PostgreSQL auth-session lock also used by logout and token rotation/revocation. An insertion that wins that lock may complete before logout; a revocation that commits first prevents the new insertion. Idempotent replay checks are outside this insertion lock, analysis/provider requests are not covered, and session/token identity still does not prove SMS ownership. Current mobile paths continue using legacy routes. The change is pending hosted and full migrated-schema verification.

The Flutter API client also fences observed session transitions and requires a known same-client rotation lineage before retrying an expired request. It checks the token pair before refreshing or cleaning up, but its read/check/write and read/check/clear sequences are not atomic, already-started requests cannot be recalled, and other engines can bypass the in-memory generation. These checks do not establish message ownership.

## Requirements for wiring

1. Introduce one authoritative native session/capture fence. Serialize session activation, invalidation and snapshot/capture with the same native authority, in the app process; document and test the manifest/process assumption. Establish a clear durable linearization point before sign-out/switch proceeds to network work. If invalidation or a durable write fails, do not claim a successful safe transition or guess an owner. Avoid treating a saved credential, sender or cached Flutter state alone as proof of an active session. Coordinate all auth paths, app restoration, enable/disable, failures, stale responses and overlapping account operations.
2. Capture and seal records with the owner from the validated snapshot, including the immutable item ID/queue key. Do not expose a general API that lets Flutter choose an owner and encrypt arbitrary data as an owned capture. Writes must be durable before source acknowledgment; include restart, failure and race tests. Apply the same capture boundary to notifications. A scoped wallet/bank sender filter is not authentication and needs separate consent/privacy review.
3. Move queue mutations, transformations and acknowledgments through a common concurrency authority shared by Android and Flutter. Carry the authenticated owner forward when a candidate becomes a review or transaction; reseal for the destination key. Compare owner and the live account before every disclosure or operation, protect in-flight responses and account transitions, use the strict server endpoints, and never delete/reassign an item on owner mismatch, 404 or 428. Ensure queue key and inner item identity cannot diverge.
4. Lock all legacy, malformed, unknown-version and unauthenticated records: no automatic display, upload, reassignment, retention deletion or retry. Preserve opaque source bytes and avoid inferred ownership. Audit both runtimes' candidate expiry, quarantine recovery, review confirm/discard, notification and rejected-transaction paths, including concurrent work. Do not introduce a blanket SMS capture/processing pause as a shortcut; ship the compatible capture and processing paths together with the legacy lock.
5. Only after isolation works, build a safe quarantined-record interface. Scope any display/action to a provable owner; handle unknown-owner entries without exposing content or auto-resending/deleting them. Define explicit, informed recovery choices separately.
6. Test A/B switches at capture, write, analysis, review, confirmation, network response and retention boundaries; logout, slow and failed invalidation, stale credentials, process restart and crash; mixed client/server versions; concurrency between receiver and Flutter; malformed and tampered records; and a real Android Keystore/device. The simulated SMS tests must not use real user messages. Provider privacy/consent and the Android Focus Shield device checks remain separate release requirements.
